# Draft limitation: the 500-employee threshold is a different policy from the one that was repealed

For the report's limitations section. Marlowe asked for this on 28 Sep.

**Draft text**

The historical evidence in this report comes from a tax that applied at 50 employees and cost $4 per employee per month, so crossing the threshold cost a firm roughly $2,400 a year, about 0.1 percent of the payroll of a fifty-person employer. The proposal applies at 500 employees and $33 per employee per month, so crossing the threshold would cost roughly $198,000 a year. The incentive to stay below the threshold is about eighty times larger, and the firms facing it are different. Among Chicago firms in 2025, those at 500 or more have a median of 900 employees and 20 Chicago locations on average, and 76 percent have more than one location; firms at 50 or more have a median of 82 employees and 3 locations, and 24 percent have more than one. Our test of bunching below the old threshold can observe only single-location businesses that report their own headcount, so it says nothing about the multi-location firms that make up most of the 500 population. We find no measurable response to the old tax at 50. That finding does not transfer to 500, and the report makes no claim about how firms at 500 would behave.

**What the report can say about 500**

- Descriptive only: 243 firms and 388,336 employees are at or above 500 in 2025; 10 firms report exactly 500 and 27 sit between 501 and 550.
- Twenty-five of those firms fall below 500 once employees who usually work from home and live outside the city are removed (58 if every remote worker lives outside), and 96 percent of them are within 550 employees. That is a mechanical property of the threshold and the survey rates, not observed behaviour.
- Geographic portability is reported as three nested definitions (finance only; adding information, professional and technical services and management of companies; adding real estate, administrative services and wholesale), covering 10, 30 and 37 percent of base employment. Revenue at risk from remote work is $1M, $4M and $5M across the three. A purely rate-based rule (every sector above the metro work-from-home rate) selects exactly the broad definition.

**Do not claim**

- That the null at 50 predicts the response at 500.
- That the heap at exactly 500 reflects avoidance. No tax has applied at 500, and round headcounts such as 400 and 600 show the same heaping.
